Why Refinish

A Fraction of the Cost of Retiling

A shower with worn tile, a dull pan, or hairline cracks in the surround doesn't always call for a full tear-out. Retiling means days without a working shower, a contractor coordinating waterproofing, tile, and grout work, and a bill that adds up fast once demolition and disposal are factored in. Refinishing skips almost all of it — we resurface the shower pan, walls, and enclosure in place, sealed watertight and ready to use again within a day or two.

It's also the fastest way to fix the things that make a shower feel unsafe or high-maintenance: a slick pan, grout lines that never quite come clean, or a surround that's started to crack and leak at the seams.

Our Process

How We Refinish a Shower

Every shower we refinish goes through the same careful process, whether it's a simple pan recoat or a full pan, wall, and enclosure refinish.

1

Protect & Prep the Space

We mask off the surrounding floor, walls, and fixtures with plastic sheeting and tape, and remove glass doors, tracks, and hardware so overspray stays contained to the shower itself.

2

Repair Cracks, Chips & Caulk Lines

Cracks in the pan or walls are repaired, and failing caulk or grout around the seams is removed before any coating goes on.

3

Strip, Etch & Sand

The existing surface is stripped of soap scum and old coating, then chemically etched and hand-sanded — working carefully into corners and around the drain.

4

Apply Non-Slip Texture (Optional)

If requested, a slip-resistant texture is added to the shower pan at this stage, broadcast into the surface before coating.

5

Prime & Spray the Coating

We spray a bonding primer followed by multiple coats of a watertight, high-gloss finish, worked into every seam and corner.

6

Cure, Reseal & Walkthrough

Drain hardware, glass doors, and caulking are reinstalled once the coating has flashed off. We walk the finished shower with you and confirm exactly when it's safe to use.

Non-Slip Texture

A Simple Upgrade That Prevents Falls

A freshly refinished shower pan is smooth and glossy — which also means it can be slicker underfoot than the surface it replaced. For households with young kids, older family members, or anyone concerned about slipping, we can add a textured, slip-resistant finish to the pan as part of the same visit.

The texture is broadcast into the final coat, so it becomes a permanent part of the surface rather than a separate mat or treatment that wears off or needs replacing. It adds grip underfoot without changing the color or feel of the rest of the shower, and it cleans the same way the surrounding surface does.

It's a popular add-on for aging-in-place bathrooms, rental units, and any household that's had a close call on a wet shower floor — and it costs far less than retrofitting a shower with a new textured base.

Care & Maintenance

Caring for Your Refinished Shower

A refinished shower is durable, but how it's cleaned in the weeks after service matters — here's how to keep it looking new.

  • Wait before use. Give the coating its full cure window — usually 24 to 48 hours — before running water.
  • Squeegee after each use. Wiping down the walls and pan cuts down on soap scum and mineral buildup, keeping the finish glossy longer.
  • Skip abrasive cleaners. Scouring powders, steel wool, and harsh abrasive pads will dull a refinished surface over time. Stick to non-abrasive liquid cleaners and a soft cloth or sponge.
  • Choose bath mats carefully. Suction-cup or rubber-backed mats left in place can trap moisture against the surface — lift and dry them after use.
  • Re-caulk the perimeter as needed. Caulking around the shower's edges wears faster than the coating itself — keeping it intact protects the seal underneath.
