Why Cultured Marble Is Different

Not Stone, Not Quartz — and Not Every Refinisher's Specialty

Cultured marble is a manufactured surface: crushed limestone or marble dust bound in a polyester resin, cast into a mold, and topped with a thin gel coat that gives it its shine. That makes it structurally different from natural stone, quartz, porcelain, and acrylic — the surfaces most refinishing companies build their whole process around.

The gel coat responds differently to stripping and etching than an acrylic tub does, and the thermal-shock cracks cultured marble is known for — hairline cracks from hot water hitting a cold surface, or vice versa — need a specific structural repair before any coating goes on. Skip that step, or use the wrong approach, and the repair fails within months, which is exactly why a lot of refinishers pass on cultured marble jobs rather than risk it.

It's a surface we've worked with for years, on tubs, integrated sink-and-vanity units, shower surrounds, and countertops across Calgary. If you've called around and been turned down, or had a "quick fix" fail on you before, that's usually why — and it's the gap we specialize in closing.

Our Process

How We Refinish Cultured Marble

Cultured marble gets a process built around what actually makes the material different — not the same steps we'd use on an acrylic tub or a porcelain sink.

1

Protect & Prep the Space

We mask off the surrounding floors, walls, and fixtures with plastic sheeting and tape so overspray and dust stay contained to the piece itself.

2

Assess & Structurally Repair Cracks

Thermal-shock cracks and chips are identified and structurally repaired first — filled and reinforced so the repair holds under the coating, not just cosmetically patched over.

3

Strip & Prep the Gel Coat

The existing gel coat is stripped and prepped with the specific process cultured marble needs, distinct from the prep an acrylic or porcelain surface gets.

4

Color & Pattern Match

The base color, and any veining or pattern in the surface, is matched so repaired areas blend into the surrounding gel coat.

5

Prime & Spray the Coating

We spray a bonding primer followed by multiple coats of a high-gloss finish engineered to bond with cultured marble's gel coat chemistry.

6

Cure, Reinstall & Walkthrough

Once the final coat is on and cured, any hardware or fittings are reinstalled. We walk the finished piece with you and confirm exactly when it's safe to use.

Care & Maintenance

Keeping Refinished Cultured Marble Looking New

A little care goes a long way — especially since the way cultured marble is treated day to day is often what caused the original damage.

  • Wait before use. Give the coating its full cure window — usually 24 to 48 hours — before running water.
  • Skip abrasive cleaners. Scouring powders, steel wool, and harsh abrasive pads will dull a refinished surface over time. Stick to non-abrasive liquid cleaners and a soft cloth or sponge.
  • Avoid extreme temperature swings. Don't pour boiling water directly onto a cold surface, or run cold water onto one that's just been in the heat — this is what causes thermal-shock cracking in the first place.
  • Don't let water sit. Cultured marble's gel coat holds up better when standing water is wiped away rather than left on the surface for long periods.
  • Avoid dropping heavy or sharp objects. The coating resists everyday wear well, but a dropped bottle or razor can still chip it, just like it could a factory finish.
