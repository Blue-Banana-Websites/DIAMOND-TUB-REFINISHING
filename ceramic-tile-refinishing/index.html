Why Refinish

A Fraction of the Cost, a Fraction of the Time

Tearing out tile means days of demolition, dust that spreads well past the bathroom, and a bill that climbs fast once you're paying for removal, disposal, new tile, and reinstallation. Refinishing skips nearly all of it. Instead of ripping the tile out, we resurface it and the grout lines in place with a durable, tile-grade coating — usually finished in a day and ready to use again shortly after.

For homeowners, that means no re-tiling schedule and no wondering whether the new tile will match the old. For landlords and property managers, it means a bathroom back in service fast, without a full renovation. And because the process works on the tile you already have, the substrate underneath stays untouched — which keeps both the cost and the mess to a minimum.

Done in a Day

Less Mess, Less Disruption

We protect the surrounding floors, walls, and fixtures before we start, and there's no demolition dust or broken tile to clean up afterward.

Built to Last

A properly refinished tile surface holds up to years of daily use when cared for with non-abrasive cleaners — every job is backed by a written warranty.

Our Process

How We Refinish Ceramic Tile

Every tile surface we refinish goes through the same careful process, whether it's a shower wall, a tub surround, or a full bathroom floor.

1

Protect & Prep the Space

Before anything else, we mask off the surrounding floors, walls, vanity, and fixtures with plastic sheeting and tape so overspray and dust stay contained to the tile itself. Fixtures and hardware in the way are removed at this stage.

2

Repair Cracked Tile & Regrout as Needed

Cracked or missing tile pieces, crumbling grout, and loose caulking are repaired before any coating goes on — a coating over a failing substrate never lasts, so this step comes first.

3

Strip, Etch & Sand

The existing tile and grout lines are stripped of soap scum, oils, and old sealant, then chemically etched and hand-sanded to create a profile the new coating can bond to.

4

Prime & Spray the Coating

With the surface prepped and the space fully ventilated, we spray a bonding primer followed by multiple coats of a high-solids, high-gloss finish — carefully worked into grout lines, corners, and edges so nothing gets missed.

5

Cure & Walkthrough

Once the final coat is on, the surface flashes off before hardware and caulking are reinstalled. We walk the finished tile with you and confirm exactly when it's safe to use — typically 24 to 48 hours later.

Need New Tile Instead?

When Replacement Makes More Sense

Refinishing works when the tile itself is structurally sound — cracked but intact, worn but not crumbling, dated but not failing underneath. When water damage, extensive substrate rot, or a full layout change is involved, replacement is the better call, and we'll tell you that up front.

Care & Maintenance

Caring for Your Refinished Tile

A refinished tile surface is durable, but it's not indestructible, and how it's cleaned in the first few weeks matters. Here's how to keep the finish looking new for years.

  • Wait before use. Give the coating the full cure window we recommend — usually 24 to 48 hours — before running water on it.
  • Skip abrasive cleaners. Scouring powders, steel wool, and harsh abrasive pads will dull a refinished surface over time. Stick to non-abrasive liquid cleaners and a soft cloth or sponge.
  • Avoid acidic or bleach-heavy cleaners. Strong acids and undiluted bleach can break down the coating and grout sealant faster than normal wear. A mild, pH-neutral cleaner is the safer everyday choice.
  • Squeegee after showers. Wiping down shower walls after use cuts down on soap scum and mineral buildup, keeping the finish glossy for longer between cleanings.
  • Avoid dropping heavy or sharp objects. The coating resists everyday wear well, but a dropped bottle or razor can still chip it, just like it could an original glaze.
