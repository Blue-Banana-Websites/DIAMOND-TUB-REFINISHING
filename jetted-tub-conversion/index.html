Why Service Your Jetted Tub

What's Really Coming Out of Those Jets

Jetted tubs feel great, but the same jet lines that deliver the massage are a closed network of tubing that never fully drains. Every bath leaves behind trace amounts of soap, skin oils, and shampoo residue inside the lines, and over months and years that residue becomes biofilm — the source of the black or grey flecks some jetted tub owners see floating in the water once the jets kick on. Scrubbing the tub shell does nothing for what's built up inside the plumbing.

Refinishing a jetted tub means dealing with both sides of the problem. We open, flush, and reseal the jet lines and fittings so what circulates through them is clean, and we resurface the shell itself so chips, dullness, and staining disappear too. For owners who've decided the jets aren't worth the upkeep, converting to a standard soaking tub removes the issue for good.

Our Process

How We Service & Refinish a Jetted Tub

Every jetted tub we work on goes through the same careful process, whether it's a jet line service and refinish or a full conversion to a standard soaking tub.

1

Protect & Prep the Space

Before anything else, we mask off the surrounding floors, walls, vanity, and fixtures with plastic sheeting and tape so overspray and dust stay contained to the tub itself.

2

Flush & Deep-Clean the Jet Lines

We run a cleaning solution through the jet system to break loose the biofilm, oils, and mineral scale built up inside the tubing, then flush it clear — the step that actually solves the black-fleck problem.

3

Repair Chips, Cracks & Worn Fittings

Chips, hairline cracks, and worn jet fittings are filled and repaired before any coating goes on, and given time to fully cure.

4

Strip, Etch & Sand

The existing surface is stripped of soap scum and old coating, then chemically etched and hand-sanded to create a profile the new coating can bond to.

5

Prime & Spray the Coating

We spray a bonding primer followed by multiple coats of a high-solids, high-gloss finish, carefully worked around every jet, air intake, and fitting. Custom color matching happens at this stage.

6

Reseal, Reinstall & Walkthrough

Jet lines and fittings are resealed and reinstalled — or, for a conversion, permanently capped and finished flush with the shell. We walk the finished tub with you and confirm exactly when it's safe to use.

Jets-to-Standard Conversion

Convert to a Standard Soaking Tub

Jetted tubs are a lot of upkeep for something that often only gets used once or twice a year. Between the maintenance the jet lines need, the extra electrical for the motor, and the higher risk of leaks around all those fittings, plenty of owners eventually decide the jets aren't worth it — especially in a tub they mostly use for a regular bath or shower.

Converting is simpler than most homeowners expect. We don't remove the tub or touch the surrounding plumbing — we cap and permanently seal every jet nozzle, air intake, and fitting from inside the shell, then refinish the entire surface into a smooth, seamless soaking tub. No visible jets, no motor to maintain, and one less thing that can leak or fail down the road.

It's a popular choice for homeowners planning to age in place, landlords tired of jetted-tub service calls, and anyone who'd simply rather have a clean, simple tub. And because it's done in place, it costs a fraction of tearing out a jetted tub and installing a new standard one.

Care & Maintenance

Keeping a Jetted Tub Clean Between Services

Whether you keep the jets or convert to a standard tub, how it's cared for in the weeks after service matters.

  • Wait before use. Give the coating its full cure window — usually 24 to 48 hours — before running water or getting in.
  • Run a monthly cleaning cycle. If you kept the jets, fill the tub with hot water, add a small amount of low-foaming dishwasher detergent, run the jets for 10 to 15 minutes, then drain and rinse to keep the lines from building up biofilm again.
  • Skip bath oils, bath bombs & bubble bath. These are the fastest way to clog jet lines and air intakes and undo a fresh cleaning.
  • Skip abrasive cleaners. Scouring powders, steel wool, and harsh abrasive pads will dull a refinished surface over time. Stick to non-abrasive liquid cleaners and a soft cloth or sponge.
  • Wipe air intake valves dry after each use. If your tub still has jets, this keeps moisture from sitting in the line between baths.
